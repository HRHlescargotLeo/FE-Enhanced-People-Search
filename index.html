Concept

Making the people directory answer the question.

The People page holds 423 economists across nine offices, running to thirty-four pages. It can be searched by name and job title, and filtered by sector, service, office and job title. It is an accurate record of the firm. This concept puts a layer over it that takes the visitor's problem as the query and returns the two or three people best placed to help — each one carrying the published work that shows why.

The opportunity. The directory serves a visitor who already knows the name, the grade or the office. A visitor who arrives with a problem — our grid connection has slipped to 2032 — has to translate it into a sector, a service and a seniority before the list can help, and then read job titles to guess who is right. That translation is work the site can do on their behalf, and the material to do it with — profile summaries, sector and service tags, authorship of published work — is already in the CMS.

The concept

Describe the problem, meet the team

A question field at the top of the People page. The response is a short written shortlist of named economists, each one carrying the Frontier work that shows the expertise is real, and narrowable by office without leaving the page. Every name opens the full profile. The directory itself stays exactly where it is, underneath.

  • Evidence sits with the person, not in a footnote. Each economist carries the article, report or case that earned them the place on the list — so the shortlist reads as an argument and doubles as a route into the published work.
  • It returns people to the directory. The response is a way into the 423, not a replacement for them: every name opens the profile, and the A–Z carries on below.
  • The long tail becomes reachable. Beyond about page three the directory is effectively unbrowsed. Matching on expertise rather than alphabet puts the whole firm back in play.

      • 1The page opens having been asked nothing. A visitor arrives at the directory, not at an answer: the field is empty and the full A–Z sits below it. The shortlist only exists once someone has described what they need, and Clear returns the page to this state.
      • The question does the translating. The visitor never has to convert their problem into a sector, a service and a grade. Three worked examples sit under the field as a way in; the field also takes free text and lands on the nearest of them — try typing “price review” or “antitrust”. In production the examples would change with the season's demand.
      • Evidence replaces the footnote. An earlier draft numbered each claim to a source in a side rail, which read as academic apparatus. Attaching the proof to the person instead makes the shortlist argue for itself, and turns every row into a route into News & Insights.
      • The honest empty state matters. Thomas Badger, in the merger example, has published nothing on the subject and says so — he is there on project record and service tags. A shortlist that never admits a weaker match is not believable.
      • The missing summary is designed for. Lia Alscher, in the hospital example, has no profile summary written; the row falls back to tags rather than leaving a hole. With 423 people this will be the state of a good share of the directory at launch.
      • Office is the only refine offered. Seniority and language were considered and left out: a visitor rarely knows what grade they need, and offering too many controls rebuilds the filter problem the question field exists to solve.
      • 2The directory is untouched. On landing it is the page — its own title, its count, its A–Z, filter rail and card grid, exactly as designed. Once a question is asked it steps down to Or browse everyone and the shortlist takes the top. This layer is an addition, not a rebuild, and the existing page keeps working for anyone who already knows who they want.
      • Portraits. The coloured plates stand in for the studio headshots and are keyed to each person's primary sector, following Route E of the directory concepts. Production would use the real library.

      Feasibility

      What the match is made from

      Nothing here needs a new taxonomy. Four fields that already exist, or are already planned as part of the directory work, carry the whole mechanism.

      The profile summary

      The one-line, first-person description already agreed for the profile page. It is the field that makes a person findable, and the single most valuable thing to get written.

      Sector and service tags

      The thirteen sectors and thirteen services already on every profile. The existing taxonomy carries the weight; nothing new has to be invented or governed.

      Authorship of published work

      Who wrote which article, report or bulletin. This is the evidence layer — the part that turns a recommendation into an argument the reader can check.

      Project record, where it is public

      Published cases and credentials only. Anything client-confidential stays out of the index, which is what keeps the shortlist defensible.

      Beyond the visitor

      Four things this gives the firm

      The value is not only in the shortlist. A question field over 423 profiles produces information the marketing, business development and recruitment teams do not currently have.

      A record of demand

      Every question asked is a signal. Problems that recur and match thinly show where the next hire, the next bulletin or the next capability statement should go.

      The whole firm becomes visible

      Thirty-four alphabetical pages means most profiles are never reached. Matching on expertise gives the people on page nineteen the same chance as the people on page one.

      Profile summaries start to earn

      Asking 423 people to write a sentence about themselves is easier to justify when that sentence is what makes them findable — on the card, the profile and the shortlist.

      Better-addressed enquiries

      An enquiry that arrives naming a team and a problem is worth considerably more than one that lands in a general inbox, and it reaches the right person the same day.

      Effort
      Moderate. The retrieval is the straightforward part; getting the profile summaries written and the authorship data clean is the work.
      The real work
      Editorial ownership. Who reviews what the shortlist says about a colleague, and how a person opts out of being surfaced.
      Suggested phase
      Phase two, once the directory and profile pages are live. Worth piloting internally as a find-a-colleague tool first.
